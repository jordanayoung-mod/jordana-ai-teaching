Who it is for

Operations leads, founders, and small teams who already know the process and want less manual copying, sorting, or drafting. If you are one person learning AI for your own work, the teaching sessions are the right place.

Problems I can build for

Customer inquiries

Triage what comes in, and draft replies a person still approves.

Meeting notes

Turn notes into the CRM update, the task list, or the follow-up email.

Knowledge assistant

Answers drawn from documents your team already trusts, with a way to check the source.

Reports

Onboarding

Checklists and reminders so a new person, or a new client, does not depend on memory.

How a build runs

  1. Discovery call, so I can see the process and the constraint.
  2. Scoping and a written proposal.
  3. Build.
  4. Testing with your team.
  5. Handover and training.
  6. Optional support after that.

A discovery call can go on the same calendar as teaching sessions, but it does not use one of the 40 session places.

What you need to provide

A person who knows the process, sample inputs that are safe to share, and access to the tools we connect. I will ask what must never be sent to an AI tool before anything is built.

Questions that usually come up

How long does a build take?

It depends on the process. A single draft-and-route workflow is often a few weeks. I will put a range in the proposal after the discovery call, rather than guess here.

Who owns the build?

You should own the accounts, the prompts, and the workflows in your own tools. The proposal will say that in writing.

What about our data?

We use non-sensitive samples while building. Live customer data, secrets, and regulated data stay out unless we have agreed a specific control and your own policy allows it.
